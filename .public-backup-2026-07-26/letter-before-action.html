Letter Before Action: the formal step before small claims

Owed money by a person or business? A Letter Before Action is the professional, court-recognised way to demand payment — and it often gets you paid without ever going to court.

What a Letter Before Action must include

To satisfy the court's Pre-Action Protocol, a good LBA is clear and specific. It should state:

  • Who you are and who owes the money
  • The exact amount owed and what it is for (with dates)
  • Any interest you are adding
  • A clear deadline to pay or respond
  • What you will do if they do not — issue a court claim

How long to give them

Give a reasonable, protocol-compliant deadline. For a debt owed by an individual or sole trader, the Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims expects at least 30 days. For a business-to-business debt, 14 days is common. A deadline that is too short can count against you if the case reaches a judge.

If the deadline passes

If they still do not pay or reach an agreement, you can start a claim through Money Claim Online (MCOL) for most debts under £100,000. You can usually add interest at 8% per year and claim back your court fee if you win. Many disputes settle the moment a properly written LBA lands, because it signals you are serious and ready to proceed.
