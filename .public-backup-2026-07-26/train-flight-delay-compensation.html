Train & flight delay compensation in the UK

A delayed train or flight often means money back — but only if you claim. Here is exactly what you are owed and how to ask for it.

Delayed flight? Claim under UK261

If your flight departed from the UK, or arrived in the UK on a UK/EU airline, and you reached your destination 3 or more hours late, you can usually claim fixed compensation — separate from any refund. The amount depends on distance:

Flight distanceCompensation
Under 1,500 km (short-haul)~£220
1,500–3,500 km (medium)~£350
Over 3,500 km (long-haul)~£520

The airline can only refuse if the delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances — severe weather, security risks or air traffic control strikes, for example. Crew shortages and routine technical faults usually do not count.

Delayed train? Claim Delay Repay

Nearly every UK train operator runs Delay Repay. You can typically claim from 15 or 30 minutes of delay, with the payout rising as the delay grows — up to a full single or return refund for delays of 60–120 minutes. It applies whatever caused the delay, and you claim per journey.

What you need to claim

  • Your booking reference or ticket, and the flight or train number
  • The scheduled and actual arrival times (screenshots help)
  • Your bank details for the payout
