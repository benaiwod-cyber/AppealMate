Tenancy deposit dispute: how to challenge unfair deductions

The average disputed deposit is around £355 — and tenants win a large share of adjudications when they present clear evidence. Here is how the free process works and the grounds that succeed.

1. Check your deposit is protected

By law, a deposit for an assured shorthold tenancy in England and Wales must be protected within 30 days in one of three government-approved schemes: the TDS (Tenancy Deposit Scheme), the DPS (Deposit Protection Service) or MyDeposits. If it was never protected, you may be owed up to 3x the deposit as a penalty — a much stronger position than a normal dispute.

2. Know what a landlord can and cannot deduct

  • Can deduct: unpaid rent, damage beyond fair wear and tear, missing items listed on the inventory, and cleaning only where the property is left dirtier than at check-in.
  • Cannot deduct: fair wear and tear (worn carpets, minor scuffs, faded paint), pre-existing damage, or "betterment" — charging you the cost of a brand-new item to replace an old worn one.

3. Build your evidence

The adjudicator decides on paperwork alone, so evidence wins. Gather the signed check-in and check-out inventories, your dated move-in and move-out photos, receipts, and any messages with the landlord. If there is no check-in inventory, the landlord will struggle to prove the property's original condition.

4. Use the scheme's free adjudication

Tell the landlord in writing which deductions you dispute and why, and ask them to release the undisputed amount now. If you cannot agree, raise a dispute through your scheme's free ADR. An independent adjudicator reviews both sides and decides — their decision is binding and costs you nothing.
